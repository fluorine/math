# Three-window vanilla inconsistency in a finite activity-schedule market

The structural class, observable panel and tests below were frozen on 2026-10-15 before numerical discovery. The subsequent exact family resolves outcome A within that class. Final integrated build status is recorded in VERIFICATION.md; the unrestricted-market question remains unresolved.

## Exact statement

Within the class below, fix the standing SPX strip generated by the uniform law on all 64 schedules. Thus Nk has the Binomial(k,1/2) law at every prefix k. Let ν denote Binomial(3,1/2) on {0,1,2,3}. Define

* μ1=(3,3,9,1)/16,
* μ2=(1,15,15,1)/32,
* μ3=(1,46,1,16)/64.

For **every real ε with 0≤ε<1/30**, prescribe the ith VIX call strip to be the call transform of the pushforward of (1−ε)μi+εν under n↦√(log(4/3)n/3). Every proper subset of the three strips, including the empty subset, has a structural law in this same class, and no law in this class has all three strips. For ε>0 each supplied pair witness assigns positive mass to every schedule and therefore to every raw state. The fixed SPX strip is preserved exactly. Every stated local and pairwise test T1–T4 passes.

The count-level separating functional is
2P(W1=0)−P(W1=3)−2P(W2=0)−P(W2=3)−2P(W3=0)+2P(W3=3)≤21/32.
The prescribed strip has value (22−30ε)/32, leaving margin (1−30ε)/32. This is a valid-bound claim, not a sharpness claim. The ordinary-call identification proof connects the count functional to the quoted panel. Only this explicit mixture robustness is claimed, not robustness to arbitrary price perturbations.

`certificates/exact.json` contains the three rational pair weights and the 64 pointwise slacks. `ScheduleCertificate.lean` checks the exact identities and proves their implication for arbitrary real probabilities. `scripts/exactify.py` reconstructs and checks the data externally; that Python check is discovery evidence, not a proof oracle.

## Structural class

The initial discovery class is an explicitly finite, early-revealed activity-schedule model. This is a restriction of the finite conditional-log-variance market, not a claim about unrestricted continuous markets. It is selected for its exact positive-martingale realization and its economically interpretable allocation of variance across dates, not as a floating-point discretization of latent budgets.

Events are integers 0,...,7. All time steps have length one time unit; the three windows are [2,5], [3,6], [4,7], each of tenor 3. All three share [4,5]. A schedule a=(a1,...,a6) belongs to {0,1}^6. Every probability distribution p on all 64 schedules is allowed. Independently, six fair signs are sampled; the sample space is the same 64×64 product for every law. Zero weights are allowed. The entire schedule is revealed at event 1. Sign h is revealed only at event h+1, including when ah=0. At event 0 information is trivial; at event j≥1 it is the schedule and signs 1,...,j−1. No extra information is revealed.

Initially S0=S1=1. Fix r=1/2. On cell h=1,...,6, S(h+1)=S(h)(1+r ξh) if ah=1, and S(h+1)=S(h) otherwise, where ξh is the fair sign. The same transition mechanism applies under every subpanel. A theorem for general 0<r<1 remains unproved. Time is measured in the declared unit, so the exact tenor is three; there is no claim about a literal 30-day exchange convention.

Use **integrated** budgets C(j,h), not rates. Put c=−log(1−r²)>0. Cell 0 has budget zero. For h≥1, C(0,h)=c E_p[ah], and C(j,h)=c ah for 1≤j≤h. These are nonnegative and adapted. All conditional restrictions are unnormalized full-history equations, including zero-mass histories:

* Σ_history_j pω S(k)=S(j)Σ_history_j pω for every j≤k, not only adjacent events.
* Σ_history pω [2(log S(j+1)−log S(j))+C(j,j)]=0.
* Σ_history_j pω [C(k,h)−C(j,h)]=0 for j≤k≤h.

The raw law weight is p(a)/64 on (a,ξ). `ScheduleModel.model` proves these identities from the construction; they are not realizability assumptions. At any fixed valuation date, budgets add over disjoint future intervals. A window budget is the sum of its cell budgets. Hence V_i=√(c Wi/3), where W1=a2+a3+a4, W2=a3+a4+a5, W3=a4+a5+a6. The window log identity is −2 E[log(S(end)/S(start))|history]=c Wi; its formal version is unnormalized and holds also on zero-mass histories. The idealized VIX is in volatility units; index points would multiply V by 100. No exchange settlement or continuous realization claim is made.

The class has fixed pointwise bounds: 2^−6≤S≤(3/2)^6; 0≤C≤log(4/3); and |log S|≤6 log 2. Define Γ=1+Σ_j(|log S_j|+S_j)+Σ_{j≤h}C(j,h), with eight events and 28 future budget pairs. Every law satisfies Γ²<M, where M=[1+8(6 log 2+729/64)+28c]²+1. The same bound applies to every subpanel. Standing SPX calls force all six root cell budgets to be c/2, so no initial budget is changed to fit a subpanel. These budgets are derived restrictions, not extra quotes. The uniform schedule law supplies an admissible example with strictly positive root budgets.

## Observable panel and reduction obligation

The standing SPX panel consists of every nonnegative-strike marginal call at every event. Calls and cash/forward values alone are observed. No schedule, count, budget, conditional price, or joint claim is quoted. Each designated window adds its entire nonnegative-strike VIX call strip. Removing a window removes only that strip, with all structural restrictions and standing SPX calls retained.

Let Nk=a1+...+ak. Conditional on Nk=n, the SPX distribution is that of a product of n fair multipliers 1/2 or 3/2. Prefix-count marginals therefore imply equality of standing SPX strips. Conversely, finite-support call inversion identifies SPX atom masses; unique prime factorization shows a stock value 3^u/2^n identifies n. VIX values √(c n/3), n=0,...,3 are distinct. Calls plus total probability identify their masses, including n=0. These are proved bridges, not extra observable constraints. They apply within the declared architecture and do not show that vanilla quotes force that architecture in general markets.

## Tests frozen before search

T1. Marginal call admissibility, positivity, unit SPX forward, and finite common moment bounds.

T2. Each VIX second moment equals −2/3 times the SPX expected log increment of its window. The initial cell budgets agree with the SPX log increments.

T3. Every nonempty singleton and pair of designated VIX strips has a full law in the **same** class with the fixed SPX strip. This is stronger than passing a selection of inequalities.

T4. For each pair of starts t<t′ and every K≥0, the existing overlap inequality holds:
E[(V_t²−K)⁺]−E[(V_t′²−K)⁺]≤−(2/3)E[log S(t′)−log S(t)].

These tests were not changed to fit a candidate. T4 follows from the constructed pair witnesses and is also proved explicitly for every real strike, with its right-hand side expressed through actual standing-SPX log expectations.

## Original target and outcome

The contract allowed either an exact same-class counterexample passing T1–T4 or a constructive completeness theorem. The family above supplies the counterexample outcome in the declared finite architecture, with every proper-subpanel witness and a proved separation margin. The support is the entire predeclared schedule/sign class, not an arbitrary solver grid. Extending the conclusion to unrestricted markets or continuous realization remains a separate unresolved task.

## Failed approach recorded before search

Five activity cells with the first fixing at the reveal date make the first window count a prefix count. Under the full SPX panel it is already identified (at r=1/2, spot support values 3^u/2^n identify n). It cannot supply three independent window restrictions. The six-cell timing above moves the first fixing past one activity cell. This is a model-design correction before computation, not a response to an inconvenient candidate.
