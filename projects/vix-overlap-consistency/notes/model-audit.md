# Independent model audit

Two design failures are recorded to prevent repeated search: fixing at the reveal event makes the first window count a standing prefix marginal; fixing total terminal activity makes the last window count a reflected standing prefix marginal. Both make one designated strip redundant. The frozen six-cell class avoids these restrictions.

The exact rational candidate has all 64 dual slacks nonnegative: 37 zeros, 4 ones, 18 twos, 2 threes, 3 fours. The fixed SPX count moments are the uniform-schedule prefix moments. Pair witnesses need different schedule dependence; marginal vanilla information does not identify this dependence.

The original exploratory audit computed numerical maxima of the stop-loss differences. Those maxima are not part of the theorem. The final proof instead establishes the required bound for every real strike directly from the pair witnesses and outgoing-cell budgets. Its count-unit bound is (j−i)/2; the VIX-squared bound is c(j−i)/6, equal to the standing-SPX expected-log cost. This avoids an unnecessary claim of exact maximization.

The bound21/32 follows from a nonnegative schedule inequality; the target objective is22/32. Under iid all extreme masses are1/8 and the objective is−1/4, giving the mixture margin(1−30ε)/32. Pair witnesses mixed with iid are strictly positive forε>0.

Main limitation: SPX support locking identifies count distributions inside the activity architecture but does not force arbitrary martingale transitions or discretize arbitrary latent budgets. No unrestricted-market or continuous-realization conclusion follows.
