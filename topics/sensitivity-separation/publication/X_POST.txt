EXTENDED POST

I’ve put together an AI-assisted mathematical note on sensitivity versus block sensitivity, with two refinements of OpenAI’s family-132 construction.

Sensitivity counts individual bits that can change a Boolean function. Block sensitivity counts disjoint groups of bits that can do so.

The first result gives compact deterministic tournament labels on an infinite parameter family. For words of length h, a first-difference rule forces every set whose incoming labels agree at each head to have at most h+2 vertices, whatever the tournament orientation. With h=13, this meets the construction’s forbidden-16 condition. Each edge query takes O(log² M) bit operations and O(log M) space.

The second result sharpens the profile theorem. Most candidate target repairs have to cross two nested thresholds together, and the target and gate counts constrain each other. The note proves the resulting six ordinary-profile and five joint-profile bounds.

What is inherited: first-difference coloring, the nested tournament construction, OR balancing, and composition. The additional arguments are the directed head-coherence bound and the coupled repair-counting proof. I haven’t located those exact statements in the sources checked; that does not establish historical priority.

On one fixed seed, with identical dimension and block witnesses, the certified exponent improves from 2.015410903091582… to 2.015411547311478…. Both calculations use the same no-16 label contract. The labels actually give a stronger bound of eight, which neither calculation exploits. This isolates the profile refinement, rather than optimizing the seed or claiming a record exponent. The seed dimension has 373 digits, so this is a theoretical construction.

The repository contains the PDF, full proofs, a detailed novelty audit, source pins, and exact arithmetic checks. The new proofs have AI-agent review and finite checks, but no Lean formalization or human referee review.

Corrections, counterexamples, and references to prior versions of either specific lemma are welcome.
