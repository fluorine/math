5. Legality, unrestricted replies, and scope

Freshness and ownership. The initial three body cells are Maker-owned. Cell 41 and both potential extensions, 1 and 51, are initially fresh. The second move explicitly avoids Breaker’s first claim. Every selected fork is untouched by all Breaker claims to that point and disjoint from Maker’s preceding new claims, so its centre and endpoints are fresh. No old ownership is changed or discarded.

Prior Breaker stones and fillers. All initial Breaker stones lie outside the working strip. Subsequent Breaker claims may be anywhere on the board. A claim outside the fork triples simply intersects none of them; the counting argument already includes such replies. There is no assumption that Breaker cooperates or remains within a local controller domain.

Immediate wins. After the centre claim, both displayed targets have exactly one missing cell, and those cells are distinct. Breaker can claim at most one. Maker’s endpoint claim completes the other target, and the game ends immediately, without a further Breaker response. Any earlier physical Maker win would only shorten the strategy.

Exhaustion. S12 has 76 fresh cells. The strategy uses at most four Maker claims and three Breaker claims, so there are still 69 fresh cells when the stated terminal win occurs. Exhaustion cannot interfere.

Consequence for the upstream program

The entry M41 prevents any total Breaker controller at S12—not just a controller assembled from pairing leaves. Any upstream Breaker construction must avoid allowing this position with Maker to move.

This does not contradict the accepted M32/B1 leaf. At that leaf, 1 is already Breaker-owned, and the accepted matching contains (41,51). If Maker subsequently claims 41 there, Breaker answers 51, blocking both extensions used in the argument above.

The exact distinction is therefore:

At S12, M32/B1 reaches the accepted defense, but M41 forces a physical Maker win.
At S12, M32/B1 reaches the accepted defense, but M41 forces a physical Maker win.
