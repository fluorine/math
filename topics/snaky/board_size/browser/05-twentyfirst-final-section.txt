Results and scope

There are two concrete advances.

First, after S9/M43, only B52 and B53 remain possible defensive replies. Every other legal reply has an explicit physical Maker win below. In particular, B54 loses, not merely to a matching obstruction.

Second, the supplied S11 matching leaves exactly 18 S9 targets uncovered. This gives a full-board bookkeeping reduction, an exact rule for interleaving the outer exchanges, and a finite prefix dispatcher with explicitly specified colored frontier states.

These results do not yet establish a complete defense at S9. The two resulting central states remain unresolved here.

8. Safety, fillers and the remaining boundary

For every matching leaf above, the ordinary invariant is sufficient: a pair is either wholly fresh or retired with a Breaker endpoint. A Maker claim cannot acquire both endpoints of a fresh pair at once. Hence a target with a covering pair cannot be completed before the reply.

A legal Breaker filler can hit an untouched pair and retire it. That blocks its full-pair incidences; it does not remove the obligation to cover targets using only the other endpoint. The covering argument in Sections 2 and 5 retains exactly those obligations. Completing the unpaired cells to a perfect matching gives a deterministic alternative requiring no fillers.

Exhaustion causes no exception. Immediate completion is tested on every Maker claim, and the invariant rules it out before any reply is invoked.

The resulting structural picture is:

Outer exchanges can be handled without changing the 18-defect problem. An unpaired entry permits installing 43, reducing it to seven defects. But Maker can take 43 first; that branch requires B52 or B53, followed by a genuine change to the central pairing structure.

The two states in Section 7 and the explicitly colored dispatcher frontier are the remaining correction objects. They are not yet a complete S9 defense, and none of these conditional results resolves the empty-board problem.
