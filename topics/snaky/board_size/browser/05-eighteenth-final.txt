5. Complete actual response policy

From the stated Maker-turn position, use the fixed matching \(\Pi\) throughout. No subsequent switch is needed.

After every actual Maker claim \(x\):

Check physical victory immediately.
If the board is exhausted without a Maker win, terminate.
If \(x\) is an endpoint of a pair \(\{x,y\}\in\Pi\) and \(y\) is physically free, claim \(y\).
Otherwise claim the least-ID physically free cell.

The least-ID choice is just a deterministic legal filler. It has no geometric significance.

Invariant

At every completed physical round, for each \(e\in\Pi\),

\[ \boxed{ e\cap B\ne\varnothing \quad\text{or}\quad e\cap(M\cup B)=\varnothing. } \tag{5} \]

In words: every pair is either already Breaker-hit or still entirely free.

Initially, every pair is entirely free.

Suppose Maker claims \(x\).

If \(x\) enters an unhit pair, its mate is fresh. The prescribed response hits that pair.
If its pair was already Breaker-hit, or \(x\) was unpaired, any fresh filler is safe.
A filler hitting another pair only makes that pair permanently Breaker-hit.

All pairs other than those touched retain their status. Thus (5) is inductive.

Immediate wins are impossible under this policy

Consider a target \(E\) not initially blocked by \(B_\star\). By (4), it contains a pair \(e\in\Pi\).

Immediately before any Maker claim:

if \(e\) is Breaker-hit, \(E\) cannot become Maker-owned;
otherwise both cells of \(e\) are free, and one Maker claim cannot acquire both.

Therefore no Maker claim completes \(E\). This checks victory at the correct instant, before the response.

Occupied replies and exhaustion

The policy never claims an occupied mate. A pair whose mate is already Breaker-owned is already protective, so the policy uses a fresh filler instead.

An unhit pair cannot have one endpoint already Maker-owned at a completed round, by (5). Thus the problematic case “fresh entry whose mate is old Maker-owned” cannot arise.

If no fresh physical cell remains after a nonwinning Maker claim, no Breaker response is required. If the final physical claim is Breaker’s, the game ends safely as well. There is no pass or dummy history.

Since each actual claim consumes a fresh physical cell, the finite-board game terminates. This proves a full conditional Breaker strategy, not just one-round coverage.
